Honours Research Projects (2012)

1. Simulation of water molecules in spherical boundary conditions

The problem of simulation of small molecules in a water environment is relevant among other things for molecular biology and biotechnology. Modern drugs are typically small molecules, and evaluating their energy of binding to proteins in a water environment is a first step in computational drug design.

The periodic boundary conditions have been heavily exploited in this line of research. However, they do not allow simulation of certain interesting systems - for example ions and charged molecules. The increase in the computational power, however, has renewed the interest into simulating systems under spherical boundary conditions, opening new possibilities for both theoretical advances and increasing the range of molecular systems accessible to the investigation.

The project consists in writing a simulation program on a classical mechanics level to simulate a finite water system under different models of the boundary. The student is expected to have interest in computer programming and in classical and statistical mechanics. The learning outcome of the project: the ability to handle larger pieces of simulation code, a better understanding of equilibrium fluid systems, as well as molecular dynamics simulation. The suggested reading: Allen and Tildesley, Computer Simulation of Liquids; Frenkel and Smit, Understanding molecular simulation.

For more information, contact Prof. Wang Jian-Sheng, phywjs@nus.edu.sg. This project is jointly supervised with Ivana Mihalek, Bioinformatics Institute, A*STAR. E-mail: ivanam@bii.a-star.edu.sg.

2. Electron-phonon interaction and joule heating in a quantum dot - a master equation approach

When an electronic current passes through a resistor, the resistor becomes hot and this phenomenon is known as joule heating. In this project, we model this phenomenon and related issues of energy transport in a small (one degree of freedom) electronic system using a quantum master equation approach. The project will help us to understand how heat is generated at a molecular level and how it is transported. A good understanding of quantum mechanics is required. The project is computational with certain level of programming skill, e.g., using matlab. The theory of the problem is developed in J. Thingna, J. L. Garcia Palacios, and J.-S. Wang, "Steady-state thermal transport in anharmonic systems: application to molecular junctions," Phys. Rev. B 85, 195452 (2012). The student can benefit from the interaction with graduate students who are doing research work in the related areas.

For more information, contact Prof. Wang Jian-Sheng, phywjs@nus.edu.sg.

Both projects are theoretical/computational.
