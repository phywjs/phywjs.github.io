\problem [2-4] Write sequences of {\tt first}s and {\tt rest}s
that will pick the symbol {\tt pear} out of the following expressions:
\smallskip
\ll{(apple orange pear grapefruit)}
\ll{((apple orange) (pear grapefruit))}
\ll{(((apple) (orange) (pear) (grapefruit)))}
\ll{(apple (orange) ((pear)) (((grapefruit))))}
\ll{((((apple))) ((orange)) (pear) grapefruit)}
\ll{((((apple) orange) pear) grapefruit)}
\smallskip

\problem [4-2] Define {\tt palindromep}, a predicate that tests its
argument to see if it is a list that has the same sequence of symbols
when read from right to left as when it is read from left to right:
\smallskip
\ll{(palindromep '(a b c b a)) $\quad\rightarrow\quad$ T}
\ll{(palindromep '(a b c c b a)) $\quad\rightarrow\quad$ T}
\ll{(palindromep '(a b c c c a)) $\quad\rightarrow\quad$ NIL}

\problem  What do you expect Mathematica to give you when you enter the
following expressions?
\smallskip
\ll{x \hfill \rm enter {\tt x} and return, not value is assigned to it\t}
\ll{f[x] \hfill \rm assuming that no definition is given to {\tt f}\t}
\ll{x // f}
\ll{f @ x}
\ll{x // f[\#]\&}
\ll{x /. x -> y \hfill \rm assuming that no value are assigned to {\tt x} and 
{\tt y}\t}
\ll{x //. x -> y}
\ll{x = a; x}
\ll{?x \hfill \rm after the previous line\t}
\ll{x\char`\^2\char`\^3}
\ll{z /. \char`\{z->1, z->2\char`\}}
\ll{z /. \char`\{\char`\{z->1\char`\}, \char`\{z->2\char`\}\char`\}}
\ll{\char`\{1, 2, 3\char`\}\char`\^2}
\ll{\char`\{1, 1, -1\char`\}.\char`\{1, -2, -1\char`\}}
\medskip

\hangindent=\parindent\hangafter=1\noindent {\bf Stephen Wolfram} (1959--\ ) 
Born in London, he was educated at Eton, Oxford and Caltech. He received
a Ph.D.~in theoretical physics from Caltech at an early age of 20.
After two years on the faculty at Caltech and four years at the
Intitute for Advanced Study in Princeton, Wolfram moved to the
University of Illinois, to become Director of the Center for
Complex Systems Research, and Professor of Physics, Mathematics,
and Computer Science.  In 1980 he led the development of the SMP 
computer algebra system, a forerunner of Mathematica.  He founded
