\problem We want to be familiar ourselves to arithmetics for the three
symbolic systems (Maple, Mathematica, and Lisp).  We can use them as
pocket calculators.  Which of the systems do you think is most 
user-friendly?  Consider entering the following mathematical expressions
to each of the system and watch the reaction of the systems:
$$\halign{\h$#$\hfil&\h$#$\hfil&\h$#$\hfil\cr
1 + 2, &  2-3\times5 -1/2, & 2/3 - 0.5, \cr
\sqrt{4}, & i^2, &  2^{10}.\cr}$$

\problem All the three symbolic systems (Maple, Mathematica, and Lisp)
can handle arbitrarily large integers without overflow. Try the
following:
$$\halign{\h$#$\hfil&\h$#$\hfil&\h$#$\hfil\cr
2^{100}, & 30!, & 123456789*987654321. \cr}$$
Note that the factorial operator in {\tt clisp} is {\tt !}.

\problem Maple and Mathematica can do arbitrarily precise floating-point
computation. In Maple we use {\tt evalf( )}, and in Mathematica it is 
{\tt N[ ]}.  Find the numerical values of the following numbers accurate
to 30 decimal places (quad precision):
$$\halign{\h$#$\hfil&\h$#$\hfil&\h$#$\hfil\cr
\pi, &  e, & \sqrt{2}, \cr
355/113, & \sin {\pi\over7}, & e^{\pi \sqrt{163}}.  \cr}$$
In Mathematica, make the assignment {\tt pi = N[Pi,30]}.  What is the
digits of precision of {\tt 2*pi}, and what is {\tt 2.0*pi}?  Why the
second expression gives you only six digits?  How to remove the symbol
{\tt pi} from the system when you have finished using it?

\problem In this exercise we like to see how Maple and Mathematica deal with
symbols.  What happens when the following mathematical expressions are
typed in into symbolic systems:
$$\halign{\h$#$\hfil&\h$#$\hfil&\h$#$\hfil\cr
\pi, &  x+y, & x-x, \cr
2x\times x+1 & (1+x)^2 & \sin {\pi\over 2}.  \cr}$$
Note that $\pi$ should be typed in as the constant {\tt Pi}, not a numerical
approximation.          

\problem Use Maple or Mathematica and consider the cubic equation
$$ f(x) = x^3 + 6 x^2 + 9 x + 1 = 0. $$
\itemitem{(a)}  Make a plot to see how many real roots this equation
has.  Read from the plot the rough values of the solutions.
\itemitem{(b)} Solve the equation numerically.
\itemitem{(c)} Solve the equation symbolically.  The resulting expressions
are complicated with imaginary number $i$.   But we know the solution 
has to be real (from the plot).  Simplify the expressions by hand. 
\itemitem{(d)} Solve the equation by hand if you could.  You need to
use the method of Tartaglia or Cardano.

{\ninerm 

\hangindent=\parindent\hangafter=1\noindent {\bf Niccol\`o Tartaglia} (c. 1500-1557) Italian mathematician noted
for his discovery in 1535 of the long-sought solution to the general
cubic equation.  He also wrote the influential three-volume
{\ninesl General trattato di numeri et misure} (Treatise on 
Numbers and Measures) as well as being the first, in 1543, to translate
Euclid into a modern Western language, namely his native Italian.

\medskip

\hangindent=\parindent\hangafter=1\noindent {\bf Girolamo Cardano} (1501-1576) Italian mathematician, physician,
and astrologer noted for the first publication of the solution to the 
general cubic equation in his book on algebra {\ninesl Ars magna} 
(Great Art).
The solution was in fact found by Tartaglia and had been revealed in
confidence.  Although Cardano credited Tartaglia with the discovery,
the revelation led to a bitter dispute between the two.
