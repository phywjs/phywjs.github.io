\problem Write a set of functions in Mathematica to decide whether a 
certain finite set with an operation $\cdot$ satisfies certain algebraic
law.  I have written the functions for {\sl closeness}, {\sl associativity},
{\sl commutativity}, existence of {\sl identity} and {\sl inverse}, and
have built a predicate for group.  You can use these if you want to.
Specifically, 
\itemitem{(a)} Implement the predicate for distribution law of
operation $\cdot$ over $+$. Here $\cdot$ and $+$ are just two arbitrary 
operations, not necessarily multiplication and addition.
\itemitem{(b)} Implement the predicate for zero divisor (Does 
elements $a$ and $b$ exists such that
$a\neq0$ and $b\neq0$ but $a \cdot b = 0$ ?).
\itemitem{(c)} Using the more primitive predicates to implement the 
predicates {\tt ringQ}, {\tt integraldomainQ} and {\tt fieldQ}. 
\itemitem{(d)} Is it a ring, an integral domain, a field, respectively for 
$(Z_8; +, \cdot)$, $(Z_{13}; +, \cdot)$,
and $S=\{0,1,2,3\}$ with the following addition and multiplication tables?
$$\halign{\quad#\quad&\strut\vrule\quad#&\quad#&\quad#&\quad#&\hskip 1cm
\quad#\quad&\strut\vrule\quad#&\quad#&\quad#&\quad#\cr
$+$ & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 &      $\cdot$ & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 \cr
\noalign{\hrule}
0   & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 &       0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \cr
1   & 1 & 0 & 3 & 2 &       1 & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 \cr
2   & 2 & 3 & 0 & 1 &       2 & 0 & 2 & 3 & 1 \cr
3   & 3 & 2 & 1 & 0 &       3 & 0 & 3 & 1 & 2 \cr
}$$

\problem Write in Mathematica the Extended Euclidean Algorithm for polynomial
over the field $Z_p$ where $p$ is a prime (i.e. EEA for $Z_p[x]$). 
$Z_p$ is the set $\{0,1,2,\ldots,
p-1\}$ with addition and multiplication modulo the prime number $p$.   The
pseudo-code for Extended Euclidean algorithm is,
\medskip
{\parskip=0pt\obeylines
{\bf procedure} EEA$(a, b, p)$
\t ${\bf c} \leftarrow (a, 1, 0)$
\t ${\bf d} \leftarrow (b, 0, 1)$
\t {\bf while} $d_1 \neq 0$ {\bf do} $\{$
\t\t $q \leftarrow {\rm quo}(c_1, d_1)$
\t\t ${\bf r} \leftarrow {\bf c} - q {\bf d}$
\t\t ${\bf c} \leftarrow {\bf d}$
\t\t ${\bf d} \leftarrow {\bf r}$ $\}$
\t {\bf return} $\bigl({\rm n}(c_1), {c_2 /{\rm u}(c_1)}, {c_3/{\rm u}(c_1)}\bigr)$
{\bf end}
}
\medskip
\item{} Where the {\bf baldface} letter signifies a three-dimensional vector.
Each of the element in the vector and $a$, $b$, and $q$ are in $Z_p[x]$. 
${\rm quo}(a,b)$ returns the quotient of polynomial division; ${\rm n}(a)$
is the normal part of $a$ and ${\rm u}(a)$ is the unit part of $a$. 
The return value of EEA is $({\rm GCD}(a,b), s, t)$ such that
${\rm GCD}(a, b) = s a + t b $.

\hangindent=\parindent\hangafter=1\noindent {\bf Euclid} (c.300 -- 260 BC) 
Greek mathematician and author of one of the most famous texts in
the whole of mathematics, {\ninesl Elements}.  In 13 books it covers
the geometry of the triangle, the circle, various quadrilaterals, Eudoxus'
theory of proportion, elementary number theory, irrationals, and solid
geometry.  The treatment throughout is axiomatic and based upon definitions,
postulates, and `common notions'.  Important results established include
the infinity of the primes, the fundamental theorem of arithmetic,
Pythagoras' theorem, the Euclidean algorithm, the existence of irrational
numbers, and the construction of the five Platonic solids.  Despite
difficulties with the fifth postulates, {\ninesl Elements} survived
unquestioned until the 19th century when the non-Euclidean geometry
of Bolyai and Lobachevsky was formulated. In addition to several other
mathematical works, most of are lost (including a work on conics),
Euclid also wrote on astronomy, optics, and music.
