\problem The set G of {\sl Gaussian integers} is the subset of
the complex number C defined by
$$ {\rm G} = \{ a + b \sqrt{-1}: a, b \in {\rm Z}\}$$
\item{} (where we usually use the notation $\sqrt{-1} = i$).  
\itemitem{(a)} Verify that G,
with the standard operations of addition and multiplication of complex
numbers, is an integral domain.  Further, verify that G is a Euclidean
domain with the valuation
$$ v(a+b\sqrt{-1}) = a^2 + b^2.$$
\itemitem{(b)} What are the units in G?  
Give some examples of primes in G. Given some examples of associates.
Define a unit normal ${\rm n}(a)$.  Can every element in G be factored
uniquely as product of primes? 

\problem 
\itemitem{(a)} Apply Algorithm 2.1 (Euclidean algorithm) 
by hand to compute, in the Euclidean domain Q[$x$] (i.e., polynomial
with rational numbers as coefficients),
$$ {\rm GCD}(4x^4+13x^3+15x^2+7x+1,\; 2x^3+x^2-4x-3).$$
\itemitem{(b)} Same as (a) but in UFD Z[$x$] (i.e., polynomial 
with integer coefficients), with (i) pseudo-division
without content removal (world's worst GCD), (ii) Primitive Euclidean,
(iii) Subresultant polynomial remainder sequence of Collins and Brown.
Compare the efficiency/difficulty of four different methods.

\problem Do the same, if you have time, (four different methods) for GCD of
$$\eqalign{ a(x)& =  2x^6 + 5 x^5 + 7x^4 + 3x^3 + 6x^2 - 2x + 1, \cr
b(x)& =  3x^5 + 3x^4 + 6x^3 - x^2 + 3x - 4. \cr
}$$
