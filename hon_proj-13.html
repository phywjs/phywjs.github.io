Honours Research Projects (2013)

1. Spin Seebeck effect

The Seebeck effect is the effect in that the temperature gradient generates an electric current. This effect can be used to build a device to generate electric current by temperature difference, thus converting heat to useful energy. The spin Seebeck effect is the analog of the electronic Seebeck effect. A temperature difference can be used to create spin voltage in a ferromagnet, thus causes a spin current flow.

This project intends to build simple one-dimensional lattice junction model for such system and calculate the spin Seebeck coefficient (or spin current) in some simple settings. The method of nonequilibrium Green's function will be used, together with some computational work. These theoretical calculations will be relevant to the very hot topic of spintronics.

Reference: H. Adachi, et al., Rep. Prog. Phys. 76, 036501 (2013).


2. Quantum thermal transport of the phi-4 lattice models under dynamic mean-field theory

The dynamic mean-field theory is a modern approximation theory for strongly interacting systems. It uses in electronic systems are well-known, but little work has been done in phonon transport. In this project, we intend to apply this theory to the harmonic lattice with the on-site u4 nonlinear potential and calculate the system thermal conductivity. The main aim of the project is to assess the accuracy of this approach and to explore possible extension. A good foundation in quantum mechanics and some knowledge in many-body physics will be helpful.

Reference: A George, et al., Rev. Mod. Phys. 68, 13 (1996).

For more information, contact Prof. Wang Jian-Sheng, phywjs@nus.edu.sg.

Both projects are theoretical/computational.
