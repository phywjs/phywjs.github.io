Honours/MSc Research Projects (2015)

1. The analytic continuation method for the second order reduced density matrix: Is it wrong and how to make it work?

The analytic continuation (AC) method for the second order density matrix rho(2) proposed by J. Thingna, et al., has been shown to work for some models but not for other models. A method for the second order density matrix has important implications for open quantum systems and nonequilibrium transport problems. In this project, we like to embark on a possible way to correct the original proposal and propose a scheme to make it work in all cases, or at least have a better understanding of the condition needed for AC to work. This project is quite demanding on part of the student to learn the quantum master equation method for open quantum system, and good programming skill in Fortran, in order for the project going.


2. Quantum Monte Carlo simulation for the magnetic properties of EuTiO3

The perovskite EuTiO3 is an interesting system for complex magnetic and dielectric properties. In this project, we focus on the magnetic properties, which we assume it is described by a nearest neighbor and next neighbor quantum Heisenberg spin model with spin value S=7/2. After a brief review on the mean-field theories, we embark on quantum Monte Carlo simulation to determine the Neel temperature, magnetization, and entropy. The project is motivated with experimental results in Mahi's group. We intend to compare our theoretical calculation with the experimental data on a rather quantitative level. Good knowledge in programming, preferable in Fortran or C, is a "must".


3. Efficiency of heat engine of a single quantum dot

Using the two-time nonequilibrium Green's functions, it is possible to compute physical quantities such as energy and particle number transfer in a time-dependent or driven quantum dot, such as the model discussed in PRL 114, 080602 (2015). This project intends to apply our old method of NEGF to compute time-dependent transport quantities and to discuss to what extent thermodynamics still apply here. This is an advanced project requiring both good understanding of NEGF and good programming skill.


4. Numerical implementation of dilute magnetic transport theory of Haas

In 1970, Hass in his review (R C Critical Reviews in Solid State Sciences, 1:1, page 47) proposed an simple theory of transport for (dilute) magnetic systems based on Boltzmann equation. This project intends to use this theory and perhaps implemented with MATLAB to compare with a more elaborate theory from microscopic model studied by Hongfei Qiu, and compare to some of theoretical data. The emphasize will be on the effect of magnetic field to the transport properties. Computing skill may be not that critical but a good physical understanding of both theory and experiments is essential.
