Honours Research Projects (2009)

1. Is Fourier's law valid in 1D diatomic gas?

The problem of heat transport is an old one with renewed interests, partly because of relevance to nanoscience and partly because of the fundamental interests in nonequilibrium statistical mechanics. Fourier's law is usually broken in 1D systems with momentum conservation. However, the work of W John Dartnall seems to indicate otherwise for the diatomic molecules. In this project, we study the heat transport in one-dimensional diatomic gas by molecular dynamic simulation. The thermal current will be computed. The validity of the Fourier's law will be checked in greater details. One challenge here is how to design good heat baths at the ends of the 1D system.

Good background in statistical mechanics and knowledge in programming (C, or Fortran) are required. For literature reading, S. Lepri, et al, Phys. Rep. 377, 1 (2003).

2. Quasi-classical molecular dynamics for quantum thermal transport

Traditionally, molecular dynamics gives only classical result. Recently, we developed a new dynamics which can predict partially quantum results. However, this dynamics is not perfect, e.g., uncertainly and tunnelling is not treated corrected. In this project, we explore a new dynamics, known as non-Markovian dissipative semiclassical dynamics, W Koch et al, Phys. Rev. Lett. 100, 230402, (2008). We need to implement this dynamics as a computer simulation program and study its statistical properties and compare to more exact results of NEGF.

Good background in statistical mechanics, quantum mechanics, and knowledge in programming are necessary. See J.-S. Wang, Phys. Rev. Lett. 99 (2007) 160601, and J.-S. Wang, J. Wang, and J. T. Lu, Eur. Phys. J. B, 62, 381 (2008).

Both projects 1 and 2 are theoretical/computational.
