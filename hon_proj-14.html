Honours Research Projects (2014)

1. The analytic continuation method for the second order reduced density matrix: Is it wrong and how to make it work?

The analytic continuation (AC) method for the density matrix rho(2) proposed by J. Thingna, et al., has been shown to work for some models but not for other models. A method for the second order density matrix has important implications for open quantum systems and nonequilibrium transport problems. In this project, we like to embark on a possible way to correct the original proposal and propose a scheme to make it work in all cases, or at least have a better understanding of the condition needed for AC to work. A good knowledge on quantum mechanics, as well as computational skill is required.


2. Heat transport and Seebeck effect in model topological insulator

The studies of exotic materials such as topological insulator is an exceedingly hot area of current research. In this project, we try to understand the basic physics of topological insulator using tight-binding models for electrons. We study the energy and thermal transport aspect of such systems, and in particular the Seebeck effect. The theoretical and computational method will be based on the nonequilibrium Green's function (NEGF). Knowledge of quantum mechanics and solid state physics will be a good start.


(UROPS project, 4MC) Spin-wave theory for spin-spin correlation functions of EuTiO3 oxides

Oxides have complex atomic structure as well as many different type of degrees of freedom of electrons, spins, and phonons. The interplay of these degrees of freedom produces interesting and intrigue phenomena observed experimentally. In this 4MC UROPS project, we study the magnetic degrees of freedom and its influence to the electron transport properties of particular oxide: europium titanium oxides. As this material orders antiferromagnetically below the Neel temperature, spin-wave theory is not so trivial. The project intends to work out 1) the correlations under strong magnetic field where the sublattice symmetry is restored, 2) low-temperature regime where sublattice symmetry breaking need to be taken care. When the problem of the spin-spin correlation function is solved, the last step, 3) is to calculate electron-relaxation time and transport coefficients such as electric conductivity and Seebeck coefficient using the nonequilibrium Green's function formulism and Boltzmann transport theory. A good background in quantum mechanics, solid state physics, and computational methods are required.
