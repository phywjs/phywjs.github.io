Honours Research Projects (2011)

1. Correlation functions in models for thermal transport

One approach for thermal transport is based on the Green-Kubo formula of linear response theory. Here the correlation function of energy current plays pivotal role. In this honours project, we intend to clarify: (1) the role of initial preparation of the ensembles. For this, we carefully sample the initial starting states from a well-equilibrated canonical distribution through Monte Carlo samplings. (2) The definition of currents. We will use the theory of Goetze and Michel. (3) and compare the result of molecular dynamics result with the theory. This study will give us some fundamental understanding of the relation between correlation functions and thermal transport. The student is expected to have a good background in computer programming (in C or Fortran) and a good master of statistical mechanics.

References: [1] W. Goetze and K. H. Michel, "Self-consistent phonons," in Dynamical Properties of Solids, edited by G. K. Horton and A. A. Maradudin, North-Holland, 1974. [2] Statistical Mechanics, D. A. McQuarrie, Univ Sciences Books, 2000; [3] "Understanding Molecular simulation," 2nd edition, D Frenkel and B. Smit, Academic Press, (2001). [4] my notes.

2. Theory of correlation functions in lattice systems

This is roughly a part two of the first project above. Here we intend to develop a theory along the lines of Goetze and Michel and applied to simple systems, such as 1D chain models with quartic onsite nonlinearity. If possible, we also look into other possible methods such as that of Dickel and Daw. The focus will be also on the calculation of phonon life time. We will compute the correlation functions numerically for the simple models. Background in quantum theory and statistical physics are essential, and some elementary knowledge of computing (such as MATLAB) is needed.

References: Those in project 1 as well as, D. Dickel and M. S. Daw, "Improved calculation of vibrational mode lifetimes in anharmonic solids-Part I, the theory", Computational Materials Science, 47, 698 (2010).

Both projects are theoretical/computational.
